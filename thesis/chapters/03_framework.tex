\section{Theoretical Framework}
\label{ch:framework}

This section develops the theoretical apparatus that underlies the empirical
analysis. We begin from the expectations hypothesis (EH) as a null of no
predictability, introduce the \citet{cieslak2015} decomposition of yields into a
slow-moving trend-inflation component and transitory interest-rate cycles, and
build the three-factor hierarchy that organises the thesis: the \emph{local}
cycle factor $\CF_{i,t}$, the GDP-weighted \emph{global} cycle factor
$\GCF_{t}$, and the \emph{FX-adjusted} global cycle factor $\FXGCF_{t}$ for a
US-dollar investor. The final subsection states the predictive specifications that
operationalise the research questions.

Throughout, $i$ indexes countries in the G10 cross-section $\mathcal{G}$, time
$t$ is measured in months, and bond maturity $n$ is measured in years. The
empirical maturity menu is $N=\{1,2,4,5,9,10\}$ years, dictated by data
availability (\Cref{ch:data}). All yields are continuously compounded and
expressed in percent per annum.

% ===========================================================================
\subsection{The Expectations Hypothesis as a Null}
\label{sec:fw-eh}

Let $y^{(n)}_{t}$ denote the time-$t$ continuously compounded yield on an
$n$-year zero-coupon bond and $p^{(n)}_{t}=-n\,y^{(n)}_{t}$ the corresponding log
price. Under the expectations hypothesis, the long yield equals the average
expected future short rate, up to a constant term premium:
\begin{equation}
  y^{(n)}_{t} \;\approx\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\E_{t}\!\left[y^{(1)}_{t+i}\right].
  \label{eq:eh}
\end{equation}
A direct implication of \eqref{eq:eh} is that bond excess returns are
unpredictable: the compensation for holding a longer-maturity bond is not
forecastable using time-$t$ information. The one-year holding-period excess
return on an $n$-year bond,
\begin{equation}
  \rx^{(n)}_{t+12} \;=\; p^{(n-1)}_{t+12}-p^{(n)}_{t}-y^{(1)}_{t}
                  \;=\; n\,y^{(n)}_{t}-(n-1)\,y^{(n-1)}_{t+12}-y^{(1)}_{t},
  \label{eq:rx}
\end{equation}
should therefore satisfy $\E_{t}\!\left[\rx^{(n)}_{t+12}\right]=\text{const}$.
The empirical bond-pricing literature decisively rejects this restriction:
forward rates \citep{cochrane2005} and, more powerfully, macro-anchored cycles
\citep{cieslak2015} predict $\rx^{(n)}_{t+12}$ with economically large
$R^{2}$. The remainder of this chapter builds the predictors used to document
and interpret this predictability in an international setting; \Cref{ch:results}
tests them, and the EH itself returns in \Cref{sec:meth-ehmc} as a simulated
benchmark for the magnitude of predictive $R^{2}$.

\subsection{Yield Decomposition and Interest-Rate Cycles}
\label{sec:fw-decomp}

The central premise of \citet{cieslak2015} is that nominal yields are driven by
two forces: a persistent \emph{trend-inflation} component $\trendinf_{i,t}$ that
anchors the level of interest rates over long horizons, and transitory
\emph{cycles} $\cyc^{(n)}_{i,t}$ that capture business-cycle conditions, real-rate
movements, and variation in risk appetite. For each country $i$ and maturity
$n$, yields are decomposed by the linear projection
\begin{equation}
  y^{(n)}_{i,t} \;=\; \alpha_{i,n} + \beta_{i,n}\,\trendinf_{i,t}
                    + \cyc^{(n)}_{i,t},
  \qquad
  \cyc^{(n)}_{i,t} \;\equiv\; \varepsilon^{(n)}_{i,t},
  \label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
so that the cycle is \emph{defined} as the residual of yields on trend inflation,
estimated separately for every country--maturity pair by ordinary least squares.

Trend inflation is constructed as a backward-looking, exponentially weighted
moving average of realised year-on-year core inflation $\pi_{i,t}$:
\begin{equation}
  \trendinf_{i,t} \;=\; \frac{1-v}{1-v^{M}}\sum_{j=0}^{M-1} v^{j}\,\pi_{i,t-j},
  \qquad M=120,\quad v=0.987.
  \label{eq:trendinf}
\end{equation}
The window spans $M=120$ months (ten years) and the geometric decay
$v=0.987$ places the bulk of the weight on the most recent two to three years
while never looking forward, so $\trendinf_{i,t}$ is observable at $t$ and
introduces no look-ahead bias. The normalising constant $(1-v)/(1-v^{M})$ makes
the weights sum to one.

\paragraph{Implementation note.} The decomposition in \eqref{eq:decomp} is run
country-by-country and maturity-by-maturity, so the loadings
$(\alpha_{i,n},\beta_{i,n})$ and hence the cycles are local objects; the cross
-country structure enters only later, through the global factor. The decay
parameter $v=0.987$ follows \citet{cieslak2015}; \Cref{ch:robustness} examines
the sensitivity of the results to this choice and to the window length $M$.

\subsection{The Local Cycle Factor}
\label{sec:fw-localcf}

The cycles at different maturities are highly collinear but not identical.
\citet{cieslak2015} summarise the cross-section of cycles with two objects: the
one-year cycle $\cyc^{(1)}_{i,t}$, which tracks the cyclical position of the
short rate, and the \emph{average cycle}
\begin{equation}
  \cycbar_{i,t} \;=\; \frac{1}{|N\setminus\{1\}|}
                      \sum_{n\in N,\, n\neq 1}\cyc^{(n)}_{i,t},
  \label{eq:cbar}
\end{equation}
which captures the common, longer-maturity cyclical variation and attenuates
maturity-specific measurement error. The \emph{local cycle factor} is the fitted
value of the one-year-ahead excess return projected on these two predictors:
\begin{align}
  \rxbar_{i,t+12} &= \gamma_{0} + \gamma_{i,1}\,\cyc^{(1)}_{i,t}
                     + \gamma_{i,2}\,\cycbar_{i,t} + \varepsilon_{i,t+12},
                     \label{eq:cf-reg}\\[2pt]
  \CF_{i,t}       &= \hat\gamma_{i,1}\,\cyc^{(1)}_{i,t}
                     + \hat\gamma_{i,2}\,\cycbar_{i,t}.
                     \label{eq:cf}
\end{align}
Here $\rxbar_{i,t+12}$ is the duration-standardised, maturity-averaged excess
return defined in \Cref{sec:fw-returns}. Equation \eqref{eq:cf} is the empirical
analogue of the single return-forecasting factor of \citet{cochrane2005}, but
anchored in the macroeconomic cycle rather than in forward rates;
$\CF_{i,t}$ is, by construction, the component of the cycle cross-section that is
relevant for predicting returns.

\paragraph{Implementation note.} The average cycle in \eqref{eq:cbar} excludes
the one-year maturity, which already enters \eqref{eq:cf-reg} separately as
$\cyc^{(1)}_{i,t}$; $\cycbar_{i,t}$ is therefore the mean cycle over
$\{2,4,5,9,10\}$ years. This avoids double-counting the short-rate cycle and
mirrors the role of the average forward rate in the \citet{cochrane2005} factor.

\subsection{The Global Cycle Factor and GDP Weights}
\label{sec:fw-gcf}

If government-bond markets are internationally integrated, investors should be
compensated for exposure to a \emph{common} source of risk rather than for
country-specific fluctuations \citep{dahlquist2013}. To capture this, we
aggregate the local cycle factors into a single global factor, using
time-varying GDP weights so that larger economies contribute proportionally
more:
\begin{equation}
  \GCF_{t} \;=\; \sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}} w_{i,t}\,\CF_{i,t},
  \qquad
  w_{i,t} \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{GDP}_{i,t}}
                     {\sum_{j\in\mathcal{G}}\mathrm{GDP}_{j,t}}.
  \label{eq:gcf}
\end{equation}
The weights $w_{i,t}$ are recomputed each month over the set of countries that
are present in the estimation panel at $t$, so that $\sum_{i}w_{i,t}=1$ by
construction even when coverage is unbalanced (\Cref{sec:data-coverage}).
Nominal GDP is converted to a common currency before weighting. The global
factor $\GCF_{t}$ is the international counterpart of the local $\CF_{i,t}$ and
provides the natural test of market integration: under full integration, the
global factor should subsume the predictive content of the local factor
(\Cref{sec:fw-hypotheses}).

\subsection{Excess Returns: Local and US-Dollar Investor}
\label{sec:fw-returns}

\subsubsection{Local-currency returns}
The one-year excess return on an $n$-year bond for a domestic investor is given
by \eqref{eq:rx}. Because return volatility scales roughly with duration, we
follow \citet{cieslak2015} and work with \emph{duration-standardised} returns,
$\widetilde{\rx}^{(n)}_{i,t+12}=\rx^{(n)}_{i,t+12}/D^{(n)}$, and then average
across the maturity set to obtain a single, scale-comparable return series:
\begin{equation}
  \rxbar_{i,t+12} \;=\; \frac{1}{K}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}
                        \frac{\rx^{(n)}_{i,t+12}}{D^{(n)}},
  \qquad K=|\mathcal{N}|,\quad D^{(n)}=n.
  \label{eq:rxbar}
\end{equation}
The duration weight $D^{(n)}=n$ is the Macaulay duration of a continuously
compounded zero-coupon bond. The averaging set used in construction is
$\mathcal{N}=\{2,5,10\}$ years, the maturities for which a clean
one-year-ahead excess return is identified given the data menu (each requires
the adjacent shorter yield $y^{(n-1)}$, i.e.\ $y^{(1)},y^{(4)},y^{(9)}$, all
available).

\paragraph{Implementation note.} The proposal specified a yield-adjusted duration
$D^{(n)}_{i,t}=n/(1+y^{(n)}_{i,t})$. Under continuous compounding the Macaulay
duration of a zero is exactly $n$, so we adopt $D^{(n)}=n$; this also keeps the
standardisation free of the contemporaneous yield and therefore exogenous to the
return being scaled. As \citet{cieslak2015} note, the results are insensitive to
this convention, and a simple (unstandardised) average leaves the conclusions
unchanged.

\subsubsection{US-dollar investor returns}
For a US investor who borrows one year in US dollars, converts to foreign
currency, holds the foreign bond, and converts the proceeds back, the dollar
excess return adds the realised currency return and nets out the US short rate:
\begin{equation}
  \rx^{(n),\mathrm{USD}}_{i,t+12}
    \;=\; \rx^{(n)}_{i,t+12} + y^{(1)}_{i,t}
          + \big(s_{i,t+12}-s_{i,t}\big) - y^{(1)}_{\mathrm{US},t},
  \label{eq:rx-usd}
\end{equation}
where $s_{i,t}=\log(\text{USD per unit of currency }i)$, so that
$s_{i,t+12}-s_{i,t}$ is the one-year log currency return. The same
duration-standardisation and maturity-averaging as in \eqref{eq:rxbar} are
applied to \eqref{eq:rx-usd} to obtain $\rxbar^{\,\mathrm{USD}}_{i,t+12}$. The
dollar return is the object of interest for the international investor and the
input to the FX-adjusted factor below.

\subsection{The FX-Adjusted Global Cycle Factor}
\label{sec:fw-fxgcf}

A global factor built from local-currency returns ignores the currency risk
borne by an international investor. Following the logic of the FX-adjusted
global factor (FXGCP) of \citet{dahlquist2013}, we construct an analogous
\emph{FX-adjusted global cycle factor} $\FXGCF_{t}$ that targets dollar returns
directly. Let the GDP-weighted cross-country averages of the dollar return and
of the cycle predictors be
\begin{equation}
  \rxbar^{\,\mathrm{USD}}_{t+12} = \sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}} w_{i,t}\,
      \rxbar^{\,\mathrm{USD}}_{i,t+12},
  \quad
  \overline{\cyc}^{\,(1)}_{t} = \sum_{i} w_{i,t}\,\cyc^{(1)}_{i,t},
  \quad
  \overline{\cycbar}_{t} = \sum_{i} w_{i,t}\,\cycbar_{i,t}.
  \label{eq:fxgcf-agg}
\end{equation}
The FX-adjusted factor is then the fitted value from the projection of the
average dollar return on the average cycle predictors:
\begin{align}
  \rxbar^{\,\mathrm{USD}}_{t+12}
     &= \delta_{0} + \delta_{1}\,\overline{\cyc}^{\,(1)}_{t}
        + \delta_{2}\,\overline{\cycbar}_{t} + \varepsilon_{t+12},
        \label{eq:fxgcf-reg}\\[2pt]
  \FXGCF_{t} &= \hat\delta_{0} + \hat\delta_{1}\,\overline{\cyc}^{\,(1)}_{t}
              + \hat\delta_{2}\,\overline{\cycbar}_{t}.
        \label{eq:fxgcf}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Implementation note.} Equations
\eqref{eq:fxgcf-agg}--\eqref{eq:fxgcf} implement the FX-adjusted factor in the
``top-down'' manner of \citet{dahlquist2013}: $\FXGCF_{t}$ is the fitted value of
the average dollar return on the average cycle \emph{predictor menu}, not the
fitted value of a regression on $\GCF_{t}$. The latter, written as
$\rxbar^{\,\mathrm{USD}}_{t+12}=\delta_{0}+\delta_{1}\GCF_{t}+\varepsilon_{t+12}$
in the proposal, would make $\FXGCF_{t}$ an affine transform of $\GCF_{t}$ and
hence statistically indistinguishable from it. Targeting the underlying cycle
predictors instead lets $\FXGCF_{t}$ load on the dollar-return-relevant
combination of cycles and currency dynamics, and reproduces the empirically
relevant property that $\FXGCF_{t}$ is only moderately correlated with $\GCF_{t}$
(\Cref{ch:results}).

\subsection{Hypotheses and Predictive Specifications}
\label{sec:fw-hypotheses}

The three-factor hierarchy maps directly onto the research questions through a
sequence of one-year-ahead predictive regressions. Each is estimated with the
overlapping-return inference described in \Cref{sec:meth-inference}.

\paragraph{Phase~I --- Does the cycle factor predict returns locally?}
For each country we estimate
\begin{equation}
  \rxbar_{i,t+12} = \alpha_{i} + \beta_{i}\,\CF_{i,t} + \varepsilon_{i,t+12},
  \label{eq:h-local}
\end{equation}
testing $H_{0}\!:\beta_{i}=0$. A rejection establishes that the macro-anchored
cycle mechanism of \citet{cieslak2015} operates outside the United States.

\paragraph{Phase~II --- Does the global factor subsume the local factor?}
We run the horse race
\begin{align}
  \rxbar_{i,t+12} &= \alpha_{i} + \beta_{i}\,\CF_{i,t}
                     + \gamma_{i}\,\GCF_{t} + \varepsilon_{i,t+12},
                     \label{eq:h-horse}\\
  \rxbar_{i,t+12} &= \alpha_{i} + \gamma_{i}\,\GCF_{t} + \varepsilon_{i,t+12}.
                     \label{eq:h-global}
\end{align}
Under full market integration the global factor should drive out the local
factor: adding $\CF_{i,t}$ to \eqref{eq:h-global} should not raise predictive
power, i.e.\ $\beta_{i}=0$ in \eqref{eq:h-horse}. To make the comparison clean we
orthogonalise the local factor with respect to the global factor before the
joint regression, so that $\beta_{i}$ measures the incremental local content.

\paragraph{Phase~III --- Does currency risk break the model, and does the
FX-adjusted factor restore it?}
For the dollar investor we estimate
\begin{align}
  \rxbar^{\,\mathrm{USD}}_{i,t+12}
     &= \alpha_{i} + \beta_{i}\,\CF_{i,t}
        + \gamma_{i}\,\GCF_{t} + \varepsilon_{i,t+12},
        \label{eq:h-usd-local}\\
  \rxbar^{\,\mathrm{USD}}_{i,t+12}
     &= \alpha_{i} + \gamma_{i}\,\GCF_{t} + \varepsilon_{i,t+12},
        \label{eq:h-usd-global}\\
  \rxbar^{\,\mathrm{USD}}_{i,t+12}
     &= \alpha_{i} + \delta_{i}\,\FXGCF_{t} + \varepsilon_{i,t+12}.
        \label{eq:h-fxgcf}
\end{align}
Specifications \eqref{eq:h-usd-local}--\eqref{eq:h-usd-global} ask whether the
local-currency factors retain predictive power once currency risk is
introduced; \eqref{eq:h-fxgcf} asks whether the purpose-built FX-adjusted factor
$\FXGCF_{t}$ recovers the predictability that an unadjusted global factor loses
on currency-converted returns. Together, \eqref{eq:h-local}--\eqref{eq:h-fxgcf}
constitute the empirical programme tested in \Cref{ch:results} and stress-tested
in \Cref{ch:robustness}.
