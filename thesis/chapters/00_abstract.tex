\begin{center}\textbf{\large Abstract}\end{center}
% ~150-250 words. Cover: the EH puzzle and bond return predictability; applying
% the Cieslak-Povala (2015) cycle factor to G10 government bonds; constructing a
% GDP-weighted global cycle factor (GCF) and a novel FX-adjusted global cycle
% factor (FXGCF) for a USD investor; the three-phase design; headline findings
% (local predictability, global subsumption, value of the FX adjustment).

\noindent
In our thesis, we examine whether the bond return predictability that
\citet{cieslak2015} document for the United States also applies to
international government bonds. Under the expectations hypothesis, excess
returns on long-term bonds should not be forecastable, yet a large
literature shows that they are. The cycle factor of \citet{cieslak2015},
which measures the deviation of yields from a slow-moving trend-inflation
anchor, provides the clearest economic mechanism behind this predictability.
However, it has so far only been studied for the United States. We therefore
construct local cycle factors for eleven G10 markets, aggregate them into a
GDP-weighted global cycle factor, and additionally build a novel FX-adjusted
global cycle factor for an unhedged US-dollar investor. We evaluate all
three factors in three empirical phases, both in-sample and under a fully
recursive out-of-sample protocol. We find that the local cycle factor
predicts excess returns in all eleven markets with the sign pattern of the
original study and an average in-sample $R^{2}$ of $25\%$. Furthermore, we
find that the global factor subsumes the local factors in most markets and
that it is the only factor that beats the historical mean out of sample, in
ten of eleven markets. The FX adjustment, however, adds little, because
dollar conversion destroys most of the predictability and the adjusted
factor is nearly collinear with the unadjusted one. Ultimately, a timing
strategy based on the global factor raises the Sharpe ratio of a hedged
global bond portfolio from $0.31$ to $0.38$.
