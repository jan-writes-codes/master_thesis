\begin{center}\textbf{\large Abstract}\end{center}
% ~150-250 words. Cover: the EH puzzle and bond return predictability; applying
% the Cieslak-Povala (2015) cycle factor to G10 government bonds; constructing a
% GDP-weighted global cycle factor (GCF) and a novel FX-adjusted global cycle
% factor (FXGCF) for a USD investor; the three-phase design; headline findings
% (local predictability, global subsumption, value of the FX adjustment).

\noindent
Under the expectations hypothesis, government-bond excess returns should be
unforecastable; four decades of evidence show them instead to be large,
time-varying, and predictable. The sharpest economic account is the
macro-anchored \emph{cycle factor} of \citet{cieslak2015}, which detrends
yields by a slow-moving inflation anchor and forecasts returns from the
residual cycles---but the evidence is almost entirely about the United States.
This thesis takes the cycle factor to the G10. For eleven government-bond
markets over 1989/1994--2026 it constructs the local cycle factor exactly as in
the original study, aggregates the national factors into a GDP-weighted
\emph{global} cycle factor (GCF), and introduces a novel \emph{FX-adjusted}
global cycle factor (FXGCF) for an unhedged US-dollar investor. The three
factors are tested in three phases---local predictability, global-versus-local
subsumption, and the dollar investor---both in sample and under a
fully-recursive out-of-sample protocol. Four findings emerge. First, the cycle
mechanism generalises: the local factor predicts one-year excess returns with
the US sign structure in all eleven markets, with an average in-sample $R^{2}$
of $25\%$. Second, the predictability is overwhelmingly global: a single
GDP-weighted factor matches the eleven local factors and subsumes them outside a
few crisis-prone markets, of which Italy in 2010--2012 is the telling exception.
Third, currency risk is the binding constraint for the dollar investor, and the
FX adjustment helps only marginally because, in this sample, it is nearly
collinear with the unadjusted factor. Fourth, and most consequentially, only the
global cycle factor survives in real time: it beats the recursive prevailing
mean in ten of eleven markets and the global forward-rate factor against which
it is most naturally compared, and it carries genuine economic value for a
currency-hedged investor.

\vspace{0.6em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} bond risk premia; return predictability; expectations
hypothesis; cycle factor; international government bonds; market integration;
out-of-sample evaluation.
