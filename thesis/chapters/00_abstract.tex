\begin{center}\textbf{\large Abstract}\end{center}
% ~150-250 words. Cover: the EH puzzle and bond return predictability; applying
% the Cieslak-Povala (2015) cycle factor to G10 government bonds; constructing a
% GDP-weighted global cycle factor (GCF) and a novel dollar-return global cycle
% factor (FXGCF) for a USD investor; the three-phase design; headline findings
% (local predictability, global subsumption, value of the FX adjustment).

\noindent
In this thesis, I examine whether the bond return predictability that
\citet{cieslak2015} document for the United States also applies to
international government bonds. Under the expectations hypothesis, excess
returns on long-term bonds should not be forecastable, yet a large
literature shows that they are. The cycle factor of \citet{cieslak2015},
which measures the deviation of yields from a slow-moving trend-inflation
anchor, provides a coherent economic mechanism behind this predictability.
However, it has so far only been studied for the United States. I therefore
construct local cycle factors for eleven G10 markets (five sharing the euro),
aggregate them into a GDP-weighted global cycle factor, and additionally build a novel dollar-return
global cycle factor for an unhedged US-dollar investor. I evaluate all
three factors in three empirical phases, both in-sample and out-of-sample. I find that the local cycle factor
predicts excess returns in all eleven markets with the sign pattern of the
original study and an average in-sample $R^{2}$ of $25\%$. The global cycle factor subsumes the local factors in most markets
and is the only factor that survives out of sample. It
beats the recursive historical mean in ten of eleven markets, whereas the
local factor does so in only two. For an unhedged US-dollar investor,
currency conversion erodes most of the predictability. The dollar-return
factor recovers much of it in sample but retains only a small, conditional
edge in real time. Converting the surviving global cycle factor into a
strategy, a timing rule on the global factor raises the Sharpe ratio of a
hedged global bond portfolio from $0.34$ to $0.40$.
