\chapter{Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}

This chapter sets out how the factors of \Cref{ch:framework} are estimated and
how the predictive regressions are evaluated. It first describes the
construction pipeline and the predictive-regression design, then the inference
machinery---HAC standard errors, model comparison, and bootstrap intervals---and
the expectations-hypothesis Monte Carlo that calibrates what predictive $R^{2}$
should be expected under the null. It closes with the out-of-sample protocol and
a transparent statement of where the implementation departs from the original
studies.

% ===========================================================================
\section{Factor Construction Pipeline}
\label{sec:meth-pipeline}

The empirical objects are built in the layered sequence of \Cref{ch:framework},
each layer feeding the next. (i) Year-on-year core inflation is smoothed into
trend inflation $\trendinf_{i,t}$ by the EWMA \eqref{eq:trendinf} ($M=120$,
$v=0.987$). (ii) For each country--maturity pair, yields are projected on trend
inflation \eqref{eq:decomp} and the residual taken as the cycle
$\cyc^{(n)}_{i,t}$; the one-year cycle and the average cycle over
$\{2,4,5,9,10\}$ years are retained. (iii) The local cycle factor $\CF_{i,t}$ is
the fitted value of the duration-standardised, maturity-averaged excess return
on these two cycle predictors \eqref{eq:cf-reg}--\eqref{eq:cf}. (iv) The global
factor $\GCF_{t}$ is the GDP-weighted average of local factors \eqref{eq:gcf},
and (v) the FX-adjusted factor $\FXGCF_{t}$ is the fitted value of the
GDP-weighted average dollar return on the GDP-weighted average cycle predictors
\eqref{eq:fxgcf-agg}--\eqref{eq:fxgcf}. The cycle factors are deliberately
\emph{generated regressors}: the same estimation choices that build the factor
are repeated, recursively, in the out-of-sample exercise of
\Cref{sec:meth-oos}, so that no future information enters the real-time factor.

\section{Predictive-Regression Design}
\label{sec:meth-design}

All predictive regressions forecast the one-year-ahead excess return from
time-$t$ predictors,
\begin{equation}
  \rxbar_{i,t+12} \;=\; a + b'\,x_{t} + \varepsilon_{i,t+12},
  \label{eq:meth-pred}
\end{equation}
where $x_{t}$ collects the relevant cycle factor(s) and the dependent variable
is the duration-standardised, maturity-averaged return of \eqref{eq:rxbar} (or
its dollar counterpart). Because the data are monthly but the forecast horizon
is twelve months, the regressions use \emph{overlapping} returns: consecutive
observations share eleven months of the realised return and the residual
$\varepsilon_{i,t+12}$ is therefore serially correlated by construction as an
MA(11) process. This overlap is the central econometric complication and the
reason ordinary least squares standard errors are badly biased; it motivates the
inference choices of the next section. The specific specifications---local
\eqref{eq:h-local}, the global-versus-local horse race
\eqref{eq:h-horse}--\eqref{eq:h-global}, and the dollar-investor regressions
\eqref{eq:h-usd-local}--\eqref{eq:h-fxgcf}---are estimated by OLS, with the
slope point estimates reported alongside the robust inference below.

\section{Statistical Inference}
\label{sec:meth-inference}

\subsection{HAC standard errors for overlapping returns}
The headline $t$-statistics use heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation
-consistent (HAC) standard errors of the Newey--West form
\citep{neweywest1987}, with a bandwidth of $18$ lags. The bandwidth comfortably
exceeds the $11$-month overlap, so it captures the induced serial correlation in
$\varepsilon_{i,t+12}$; $18$ lags is the convention adopted for the
twelve-month-horizon regressions throughout. For a fitted regression we report
the slope estimate $\hat b$, its HAC standard error, and the $t$-ratio
$\hat b/\mathrm{se}(\hat b)$.

\paragraph{Reverse-regression caveat.} \citet{cieslak2015} report $t$-statistics
from the reverse-regression delta method of \citet{weiwright2013}, which pairs
backward-summed regressors with \emph{one-period} (monthly) return innovations.
That estimator requires the underlying monthly returns; the constructed panel
stores only the twelve-month overlapping returns, so the genuine
reverse-regression form is not identified from the available data. We therefore
report Hansen--Hodrick/Newey--West HAC $t$-statistics---the standard robust
inference for overlapping returns---and treat them as such. The signs,
magnitudes, and the $R^{2}$, Wald, and BIC structure are faithful to the
original; only the exact reverse-regression $t$-values, which would require the
paper's appendix, differ. This is stated openly because HAC and reverse
-regression $t$-statistics can diverge for highly persistent regressors, a point
revisited in \Cref{ch:discussion}.

\subsection{Joint significance: the Wald test}
For multi-regressor specifications we report a joint Wald test that all slopes
are zero. With slope vector $\hat b_{s}$ (intercept excluded) and the
corresponding HAC covariance block $V_{s}$,
\begin{equation}
  W \;=\; \hat b_{s}'\,V_{s}^{-1}\,\hat b_{s}
  \;\sim\; \chi^{2}_{k},
  \label{eq:meth-wald}
\end{equation}
with $k$ the number of slopes, and we report $W$ with its $\chi^{2}$ p-value.
The same statistic, applied to the orthogonalised local factor in the horse
race \eqref{eq:h-horse}, measures whether the local factor adds explanatory
power beyond the global factor.

\subsection{Model comparison: BIC relative probabilities}
To compare the competing predictor sets we use Bayesian information criteria
expressed as relative probabilities, following \citet{cieslak2015}. For model
$i$ estimated on a common sample of length $T$ with $n_{i}$ slope regressors,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{BIC}_{i} \;=\; \ln\!\big(\hat\sigma^{2}_{i}\big)
     + \ln(T)\,\frac{n_{i}}{T},
  \qquad \hat\sigma^{2}_{i} = \mathrm{SSE}_{i}/T,
  \label{eq:meth-bic}
\end{equation}
and the relative probability of model $i$ is
$\exp\!\big\{(\mathrm{BIC}_{\min}-\mathrm{BIC}_{i})/2\big\}$, normalised so that
the lowest-BIC model maps to $1.0$ and the others to $(0,1]$. This rewards
predictive fit while penalising parameter count, and makes the parsimony of the
two-regressor cycle factor directly comparable to the six-yield specification.

\subsection{Small-sample intervals: the stationary block bootstrap}
Finite-sample distributions are obtained from the stationary block bootstrap of
\citet{politis1994}, which resamples contiguous blocks of random
(geometrically distributed) length with mean $L$, wrapping around the sample
end. The mean block length is set to at least the twelve-month overlap so that
the resampled series preserve the serial dependence of the overlapping returns.
Two quantities are bootstrapped:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item the predictive $t$-statistic ($L=12$), refitting $\rxbar\sim\CF$ on each
    pseudo-sample and studentising the slope with the same $18$-lag HAC standard
    error as the headline estimate, reported as a $[5\%,95\%]$ interval; and
  \item the adjusted $R^{2}$ ($L=18$), refit by OLS on each pseudo-sample, with
    its $[5\%,95\%]$ interval, following the
    \citet{dahlquist2013} convention for the predictability tables.
\end{itemize}
Matching the bootstrap HAC bandwidth to the headline bandwidth is essential:
ignoring the overlap inside the bootstrap would spuriously inflate the
$t$-statistic.

\section{Expectations-Hypothesis Monte Carlo}
\label{sec:meth-ehmc}

A predictive $R^{2}$ is only informative relative to what the expectations
hypothesis would itself produce in a finite sample. Even under the EH---zero
risk premia---realised twelve-month returns are non-zero because they embed the
future yield $y_{t+12}$, so the in-sample predictive $R^{2}$ has a
non-degenerate null distribution. We characterise that distribution by Monte
Carlo, following \citet{cieslak2015}, Section~1.

The data-generating process has two AR(1) state variables, trend inflation
$\tau_{t}$ (persistence $\phi_{\tau}$) and a real factor $r_{t}$ (persistence
$\phi_{r}$), driving the nominal short rate
$i_{t}=\delta_{0}+\delta_{\tau}\tau_{t}+\delta_{r}r_{t}$ with
$\delta_{0}=0,\ \delta_{\tau}=1.43,\ \delta_{r}=1$. Under the EH the $n$-year
yield is the average expected future short rate and hence affine in the states,
\begin{equation}
  y^{(n)}_{t} = \delta_{0}
     + \delta_{\tau}\,\tau_{t}\,\bar{A}(\phi_{\tau},N)
     + \delta_{r}\,r_{t}\,\bar{A}(\phi_{r},N),
  \qquad
  \bar{A}(\phi,N) = \frac{1-\phi^{N}}{(1-\phi)\,N},
  \label{eq:meth-eh}
\end{equation}
with $N=12n$ months. Innovation variances are calibrated so that
$\mathrm{sd}(\tau)=1.90\%$ and the one-year cycle has $\mathrm{sd}=1.74\%$; the
latter pins $\sigma_{r}$ through the one-year yield's loading on $r$, since the
one-year cycle is the part of $y^{(1)}$ orthogonal to $\tau$. Each draw
simulates the states (with a burn-in), builds EH yields on the maturity menu,
forms the duration-standardised maturity-averaged return $\rxbar$ exactly as in
\Cref{ch:framework} ($D^{(n)}=n$, $n\in\{2,5,10\}$), regresses it on trend
inflation and the real factor, and stores the adjusted $R^{2}$. The null
distribution is summarised by its $5$th, $50$th, and $95$th percentiles over
$10{,}000$ draws, evaluated on the grid of persistence pairs tabulated by
\citet{cieslak2015} ($\phi_{\tau}\in\{0.80,0.975,0.999\}$ at $\phi_{r}=0.75$,
and $\phi_{r}\in\{0.60,0.75,0.90\}$ at $\phi_{\tau}=0.975$). The predictor map is
pluggable, so the same engine can evaluate the cycle and six-yield
specifications for comparison.

\paragraph{Calibration gap.} The simulated null $R^{2}$ band is sensitive to the
exact affine mapping and to the calibration of the state variances; our
implementation reproduces the \emph{qualitative} pattern (a null $95$th
percentile rising with persistence) but does not exactly match the published
band. The gap is documented rather than closed, and the realised predictive
$R^{2}$ in \Cref{ch:results} is interpreted against our own simulated band, not
the paper's.

\section{Out-of-Sample Evaluation}
\label{sec:meth-oos}

In-sample predictability can reflect over-fitting of generated regressors, so
the factors are re-evaluated in real time. The entire chain is rebuilt
recursively using only data available at each date $t$: the yield-cycle
decomposition \eqref{eq:decomp} is re-fit on an expanding window, the local
predictive regression \eqref{eq:cf-reg} is re-estimated, and the global and
FX-adjusted factors are reconstructed from the recursive local factors. Trend
inflation is already backward-looking and is reused as is. Two distinct cutoffs
are respected: the yield-cycle residual is contemporaneous (regressors and
yields are both observed at $t$), whereas the predictive regression trains only
on observations whose twelve-month outcome has already been realised by $t$
(training dates satisfy $\text{date}+12\text{m}\le t$). Because both the factor
\emph{and} the forecasting regression respect $t$, the resulting forecast is
``doubly out-of-sample''.

Forecast accuracy is measured by the \citet{campbellthompson2008} out-of-sample
$R^{2}$ against the recursive prevailing-mean benchmark,
\begin{equation}
  R^{2}_{\mathrm{oos}} \;=\; 1 -
     \frac{\sum_{t}\big(\rxbar_{t+12}-\widehat{\rxbar}^{\,\text{factor}}_{t+12}\big)^{2}}
          {\sum_{t}\big(\rxbar_{t+12}-\widehat{\rxbar}^{\,\text{mean}}_{t+12}\big)^{2}},
  \label{eq:meth-ctr2}
\end{equation}
where the benchmark forecast is the recursive mean (a regression on a constant)
passed through the same outcome-lag training cutoff. A positive
$R^{2}_{\mathrm{oos}}$ means the factor beats the historical average out of
sample. Country results are pooled by summing the forecast and benchmark sums of
squares across countries---so each country contributes against its own recursive
mean---before forming the ratio. The same protocol is applied to the cycle
factors and to the Cochrane--Piazzesi comparison factors, giving the
head-to-head out-of-sample evidence reported in \Cref{ch:robustness}.

\section{Deviations from the Original Studies}
\label{sec:meth-deviations}

For transparency, the substantive departures from \citet{cieslak2015} and
\citet{dahlquist2013} are collected here; each is revisited where it bears on
the results.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Maturity menu.} Yields are available at $\{1,2,4,5,9,10\}$ years
    rather than the finer grid of the original papers; individual excess returns
    are formed for $n\in\{2,5,10\}$, the maturities with an observed adjacent
    shorter yield.
  \item \textbf{Sample.} The G10 panel spans 1989/1994--2026
    (\Cref{sec:data-coverage}), shorter than and not contemporaneous with the
    original US samples, so replicated levels are expected to differ from the
    published numbers.
  \item \textbf{Inference.} HAC ($18$-lag Newey--West) $t$-statistics replace the
    exact reverse-regression delta method, for the identification reason given in
    \Cref{sec:meth-inference}.
  \item \textbf{Duration convention.} Duration-standardisation uses $D^{(n)}=n$
    (continuous compounding) rather than $n/(1+y^{(n)})$.
  \item \textbf{FX-adjusted factor.} $\FXGCF_{t}$ is built top-down on the
    average cycle predictors rather than as a projection on $\GCF_{t}$
    (\Cref{sec:fw-fxgcf}).
\end{itemize}
These choices keep the construction internally consistent and reproducible while
remaining as close to the source methodology as the data permit.
