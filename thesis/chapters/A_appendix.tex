\paragraph{Construction parameters.} The following choices are fixed throughout
and are repeated, recursively, in the out-of-sample exercise of
\Cref{sec:meth-oos}.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \emph{Trend inflation.} Exponentially-weighted moving average of
    year-on-year core inflation with window $M=120$ months and geometric decay
    $v=0.987$, normalised by $(1-v)/(1-v^{M})$ so the weights sum to one
    \eqref{eq:trendinf}. The CPI history predates the yield sample, so the trend
    is fully populated from the first yield observation.
  \item \emph{Cycles.} Estimated by OLS country-by-country and
    maturity-by-maturity \eqref{eq:decomp}; the average cycle excludes the
    one-year point \eqref{eq:cbar}.
  \item \emph{Excess returns.} Individual returns formed for $n\in\{2,5,10\}$
    (the maturities with an observed adjacent shorter yield), duration-standardised
    by the continuous-compounding Macaulay duration $D^{(n)}=n$, and averaged
    \eqref{eq:rxbar}. Dollar returns add the realised log currency return and net
    the US short rate \eqref{eq:rx-usd}.
  \item \emph{GDP weights.} Annual nominal GDP converted to US dollars at the
    year-end exchange rate, divided by the cross-sectional total, held constant
    within a calendar year, and renormalised each month over the countries
    present in the panel \eqref{eq:gcf}.
  \item \emph{Maturity menu and sample.} Yields at $\{1,2,4,5,9,10\}$ years,
    month-end. The United States enters in March 1989, Japan in April 1989, the
    remaining nine markets in December 1994, all running to April 2026
    ($446$, $445$, and $377$ monthly observations respectively;
    \Cref{tab:data-coverage}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Inference and Monte-Carlo Details}
\label{app:inference}

This section consolidates the inference machinery of \Cref{sec:meth-inference}
and \Cref{sec:meth-ehmc}; it adds the precise parameter values in one place
rather than re-deriving the formulae.

\paragraph{HAC standard errors and the reverse-regression caveat.} All headline
$t$-statistics use Newey--West HAC standard errors \citep{neweywest1987} with a
bandwidth of $18$ lags, comfortably exceeding the eleven-month overlap induced by
the twelve-month forecast horizon (the residual is MA(11) by construction).
\citet{cieslak2015} instead report $t$-statistics from the reverse-regression
delta method of \citet{weiwright2013}, which pairs backward-summed regressors with
\emph{one-period} return innovations and therefore requires the underlying monthly
returns; the constructed panel stores only the twelve-month overlapping returns,
so that estimator is not identified here. HAC and reverse-regression
$t$-statistics can diverge for highly persistent regressors, which is one reason
the thesis leans its conclusions on the out-of-sample evidence
(\Cref{sec:disc-limitations}).

\paragraph{Joint significance and model comparison.} Multi-regressor
specifications report the joint Wald statistic $W=\hat b_{s}'V_{s}^{-1}\hat b_{s}
\sim\chi^{2}_{k}$ \eqref{eq:meth-wald}, with $V_{s}$ the HAC covariance block of
the $k$ slopes. Competing predictor sets are compared by Bayesian information
criteria expressed as relative probabilities \eqref{eq:meth-bic},
$\exp\{(\mathrm{BIC}_{\min}-\mathrm{BIC}_{i})/2\}$, normalised so the lowest-BIC
model maps to $1.0$; this rewards fit while penalising the parameter count, making
the two-regressor cycle factor directly comparable to the six-yield specification.
Where multiple tests are aggregated across the panel, the Benjamini--Hochberg
correction is applied (\Cref{sec:res-phase2}).

\paragraph{Stationary block bootstrap.} Finite-sample intervals use the
stationary block bootstrap of \citet{politis1994}, which resamples contiguous
blocks of random (geometrically distributed) length with mean $L$, wrapping at the
sample end. Two quantities are bootstrapped to a $[5\%,95\%]$ interval: the
predictive $t$-statistic (mean block length $L=12$, refitting $\rxbar\sim\CF$ on
each pseudo-sample and studentising with the same $18$-lag HAC standard error as
the headline estimate) and the adjusted $R^{2}$ ($L=18$, refit by OLS). Matching
the bootstrap HAC bandwidth to the headline bandwidth is essential: ignoring the
overlap inside the bootstrap would spuriously inflate the $t$-statistic.

\paragraph{Expectations-hypothesis Monte Carlo.} The null distribution of the
predictive $R^{2}$ is simulated from a calibrated expectations-hypothesis economy
(\Cref{sec:meth-ehmc}) with two AR(1) state variables---trend inflation $\tau_{t}$
and a real factor $r_{t}$---driving the nominal short rate
$i_{t}=\delta_{0}+\delta_{\tau}\tau_{t}+\delta_{r}r_{t}$; under the EH the
$n$-year yield is affine in the states \eqref{eq:meth-eh}. \Cref{tab:app-ehmc}
collects the calibration. Each of $10{,}000$ draws simulates the states (after a
burn-in), builds EH yields on the maturity menu, forms $\rxbar$ exactly as in the
data ($D^{(n)}=n$, $n\in\{2,5,10\}$), and stores the adjusted $R^{2}$; the null is
summarised by its $5$th, $50$th, and $95$th percentiles over the persistence grid.
As stated in \Cref{sec:meth-ehmc}, the implementation reproduces the qualitative
pattern of \citet{cieslak2015}---a null band that widens with persistence---but
not the exact published band, so the observed in-sample $R^{2}$ are judged against
this calibration rather than the paper's. The margin is wide, which is why the
conclusion is regarded as safe.

\paragraph{Out-of-sample protocol.} The recursive forecast is scored by the
\citet{campbellthompson2008} statistic \eqref{eq:meth-ctr2} against the recursive
prevailing mean. The entire factor chain is rebuilt at each date from data
available only up to that date; both the factor and the predictive regression
respect time $t$ (training dates satisfy $\text{date}+12\text{m}\le t$), so the
forecast is doubly out-of-sample. The baseline uses expanding windows with a
five-year minimum training span; \Cref{sec:rob-oos-scheme} reports the
sensitivity to three- and seven-year minimums and to ten- and fifteen-year
rolling windows.
